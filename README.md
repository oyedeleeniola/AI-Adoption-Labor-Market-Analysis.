# AI Adoption & Technical Labor Market Analysis

## Project Overview

This project investigates the relationship between artificial intelligence (AI) adoption within companies and changes in technical job postings, AI-related hiring, technical salaries, and AI skills requirements.

The analysis also explores differences across regions, age groups, and company sizes using interactive Power BI dashboards.

## Research Question

How does AI adoption within companies relate to changes in technical job postings and average technical salaries, and how does this relationship vary by region, age group, and company size?

## Project Objectives

- Examine the relationship between AI adoption and technical job postings.
- Analyse the relationship between AI adoption and AI-related job postings.
- Explore the relationship between AI adoption and technical salaries.
- Investigate changes in AI skills requirements.
- Compare patterns across regions, age groups, and company sizes.
- Track monthly trends in AI adoption and labour-market indicators.

## Tools Used

- Microsoft Excel — data cleaning and preparation.
- Power Query — data transformation and aggregation.
- Microsoft Power BI — interactive dashboards and visual analytics.
- DAX — analytical measures and calculations.

## Dataset Overview

The project uses a synthetic dataset containing:

- 12 companies
- 12 months of observations
- 6 AI tools
- 5 age groups

The data was cleaned and standardised before analysis. Exact duplicate records were removed, and selected labels were made consistent.

**Note:** The dataset is synthetic and should not be interpreted as verified evidence of actual labour-market outcomes.

## Key Findings

The analysis identified positive relationships between AI adoption and technical job postings, as well as between AI adoption and AI-related job postings.

The relationship between AI adoption and technical salaries was comparatively weaker.

These are associations within the synthetic dataset. Correlation does not establish causation, and the findings should not be generalised to the real-world labour market without further validation.

## Limitations

- The dataset is synthetic rather than observed real-world company data.
- Correlations do not demonstrate cause-and-effect relationships.
- Salary comparisons across countries require currency and cost-of-living considerations.
- Findings may differ when tested against real-world datasets.

## Recommendations

- Validate the findings using real-world job-posting and salary data.
- Normalise salaries across countries before making direct comparisons.
- Investigate differences by industry, job role, and experience level.
- Continue monitoring AI adoption and technical hiring over time.
